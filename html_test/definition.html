DEFINITION OF A FUNCTION

The foundational language of change in calculus

In high school math, we have an intuitive understanding of a function as a curve which represents an association between an input variable xx and an output variable yy. In this section, we will refine this understanding by precisely defining what a function is. Functions are much more than curves on a grid, and opening ourselves up to a more precise and general definition is the first step to familiarize ourselves with the variety of definitions and theorems we encounter in calculus.

Definition. Given two sets XX and YY, a function from XX to YY is a rule or mapping that takes each element of XX and associates with it a single element of YY. In this case, we write f:X→Yf:X \to Y.

XX is said to be the domain of the function ff, while YY is said to be its codomain. All elements of YY do not necessarily occur as outputs of ff, and the ones that do form a subset of YY known as the range of ff.

You are already familiar with functions: linear equations y=mx+by=mx+b and quadratic equations y=ax2+bx+cy=ax^2+bx+c are examples of functions of the variable xx, where constants are used. In this case, we write y=f(x)y=f(x). Given any input xx in the domain, f(x)f(x) produces one and only one value.

This leads us to the familiar notion of the vertical line test: at any point on the graph of the function, it must intersect a vertical line exactly once.
